A photographic record of doors in Besançon, paired with structured
information about selected characteristics: whether a door is open or
closed, whether it has a rounded top, its main colour, and the presence of
graffiti or stickers/signage.

## What does the dataset directly record?

For each photographed door, the structured data records:

- whether the door is open or closed;
- whether it has a rounded top;
- its main colour;
- the presence or absence of graffiti;
- the presence or absence of stickers or signage;
- additional notes for some photographs.

The dataset therefore supports direct comparison of colour, rounded form,
openness, graffiti, and stickers/signage across the collection.

## Who could use the dataset?

The dataset could support research in several fields.

**Art historians and architectural historians** could study selected door
forms, colour, architectural details visible in the images, and vernacular
design.

**Urban historians and heritage researchers** could examine architectural
surfaces and the visual character of urban space.

**Archaeologists** could compare architectural and visual features across
the collection and return to the photographs for closer examination of
form, possible material characteristics, and recurring patterns.

**Urban and cultural geographers** could study doors as elements of the
visual culture of urban space.

**Digital humanities researchers** could organise, search, compare, and
visualise the photographic material.

## How could the dataset be reused?

The dataset offers two levels of reuse.

**Reuse of the existing annotations**

Researchers can analyse and compare:

- colour;
- rounded form;
- open and closed doors;
- graffiti;
- stickers/signage.

**Reuse of the photographs**

Researchers can return to the original images, ask new questions, and add
further annotations relating, for example, to:

- materials;
- decoration;
- door types;
- architectural details;
- the immediate architectural setting.

This second level is an important part of the dataset's value. The
photographs remain available for further interpretation, even when a
particular feature has not yet been recorded in the structured data.

## What does location data add?

With location data attached to each photograph, the dataset could also
support spatial analysis:

- mapping the photographed doors across Besançon;
- examining the distribution of features such as colour, rounded form, or
  graffiti;
- comparing patterns in the different areas represented in the collection.

These patterns would describe the doors included in the collection rather
than constitute a representative survey of every door in Besançon.

## Possible use in computer vision

The labelled photographs could also serve as test material for methods
that automatically identify features such as:

- rounded door tops;
- colour;
- graffiti;
- stickers or signage;
- whether a door is open or closed.

This could offer an additional form of reuse at a later stage of the
project.

## Final point

The value of the dataset lies in the fact that the doors are not recorded
merely as isolated photographs.

By combining photographs with structured annotations, the dataset allows
researchers to compare selected visual characteristics across the
collection. At the same time, the original images remain available for
further interpretation and new forms of annotation.

The collection therefore becomes a research resource in its own right,
rather than simply a set of photographs.
